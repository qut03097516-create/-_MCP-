### [US DOE puts US$5.25 billion towards 23GW of grid improvements](https://www.pv-tech.org/us-doe-puts-us5-25-billion-towards-23gw-of-grid-improvements/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The US Department of Energy \(DOE\) is investing US$5.25 billion through its SPARK initiative to fund 31 grid-improvement projects across 26 states, aiming to add 23 gigawatts \(GW\) of electricity capacity. This allocation will upgrade 1,500 miles of transmission lines and deploy grid-enhancing technologies over approximately 21,000 miles, with a primary focus on reconductoring using Advanced Transmission Technologies \(ATTs\). The funding includes US$1.9 billion from federal sources and US$3.35 billion in recipient cost-share contributions. These upgrades are designed to alleviate grid congestion that has stalled renewable energy projects, including solar PV, and to support growing electricity demand from sectors like data centers.

**「Technical Details」** Reconductoring entails replacing existing power lines with higher-capacity conductors to increase grid capacity without building new infrastructure. When paired with Advanced Transmission Technologies \(ATTs\), this method enhances operational efficiency, improves reliability, and reduces costs by optimizing existing transmission corridors.

**「Industry Impact」** This investment will accelerate solar PV integration by expanding grid capacity to connect more projects, directly addressing the massive backlog of 2.6TW of solar and storage capacity in US interconnection queues as of 2023.

### [Solar PV Repowering to Account for 23% of New Capacity by 2040s](https://www.pv-tech.org/solar-repowering-account-23-new-capacity-additions-2040s/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Wood Mackenzie report forecasts that solar PV repowering will account for 23% of new capacity additions by the 2040s. This trend is driven by projects reaching the ends of their operational lives and rapid technological advancements that incentivize replacing outdated components. By the 2040s, over 2.5TW of wind and solar capacity will require end-of-life decisions, with solar module efficiencies having increased by 69% from 2005 to 2025. Further technological progress could boost cell efficiency by more than 50% by 2045, making repowering attractive due to improved generation and smaller project footprints.

rss · PV-Tech · Sep 25, 08:23

**「Technical Details」** Repowering involves expanding or upgrading existing projects with new technology to enhance capacity or output, as opposed to merely revamping old components. The solar sector sees frequent technological innovations, with new advancements emerging every other year, leading to shorter practical lifespans for assets as owners seek to capitalize on efficiency gains. Key metrics include a 69% rise in module efficiency over two decades and potential for cell efficiency improvements exceeding 50% by 2045, coupled with project sites becoming 30% smaller.

**「Industry Impact」** Decommissioning and repowering will drive more than 70% of new renewable energy installations across Europe. However, this impact is region-specific, contributing only 1% to new capacity additions in Asia.

### [Reassessing Solar Mounting: Trackers vs. East-West Fixed-Tilt Systems](https://www.pv-tech.org/to-track-or-not-to-track-2/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Dramatically lower module prices have shifted the cost structure in solar projects, making high-density east-west fixed-tilt systems more competitive against single-axis trackers for levelized cost of electricity \(LCOE\) optimization. While trackers typically offer 15-25% higher specific energy yield, east-west systems can achieve installation densities up to 2.1 MW per hectare with lightweight structures like PEG, using 70% less steel and requiring 400-500 man-hours per MWp for installation. The flatter generation profile of east-west systems allows for higher DC-AC ratios without significant clipping losses, improving inverter utilization and reducing grid constraints. Over project lifetimes, factors such as reduced construction time, lower material intensity, and operational simplicity can outweigh the yield advantage of trackers, leading to competitive LCOE under specific local conditions.

rss · PV-Tech · Sep 24, 15:20

**「Technical Details」** High-density east-west fixed-tilt systems employ low tilt angles to minimize inter-row shading, maximizing land use capacity without the mechanical complexity of single-axis trackers. The orientation produces a flatter daily generation curve, enabling safe DC-AC overbuilding and reducing clipping losses that can occur with peaky tracker output. Fixed systems eliminate moving parts such as drive mechanisms and control units, decreasing maintenance needs and failure points. Modern lightweight mounting designs, like the PEG system, reduce steel consumption by approximately 70% and simplify installation, enhancing scalability and construction efficiency.

**「Industry Impact」** This analysis may lead developers to increasingly consider high-density east-west fixed-tilt systems in projects where land costs, construction efficiency, or grid constraints are critical, potentially influencing tracker market adoption and system design practices.

### [Websol secures land for 4GW solar cell and module plant in West Bengal](https://www.pv-tech.org/websol-secures-land-for-4gw-solar-cell-module-manufacturing-plant-in-west-bengal/) ⭐️ 5.0/10

Indian solar manufacturer Websol Energy System has secured a 2.4 million square foot parcel of land at the Falta Industrial Park in West Bengal for a new 4GW solar cell and module manufacturing plant. The project will be executed in two phases of 2GW each, with the first phase targeted for June 2027 and the second for June 2028. This relocation from Andhra Pradesh reportedly maintains the timeline for the INR 30 billion \(US$310 million\) investment. The company already operates 1.2GW of PERC cell and 500MW of module capacity at the adjacent Falta Special Economic Zone, and it cites access to skilled labor and an established local supply chain as key reasons for the expansion.

**「Technical Details」** Websol&\#x27;s existing operational capacity at the Falta site comprises passivated emitter rear contact \(PERC\) cells, which are listed on India&\#x27;s Approved List of Models and Manufacturers \(ALMM\). The article does not specify the cell technology for the new 4GW expansion. The plant&\#x27;s output will include both solar cells and modules, and the company&\#x27;s current 1.2GW cell and 0.5GW module capacities are noted as already being on the ALMM.

**「Industry Impact」** This expansion contributes to the rapid growth of India&\#x27;s domestic solar manufacturing supply chain, where module nameplate capacity has surpassed 233GW, far exceeding the country&\#x27;s 65GW annual demand. The investment also reflects the ongoing effort to address the significant capacity imbalance, as solar cell manufacturing capacity \(over 40GW\) currently lags behind module capacity.
