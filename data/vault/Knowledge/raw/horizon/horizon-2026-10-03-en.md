### [US Solar Module Quality Challenges Amid Procurement Policies](https://www.pv-tech.org/navigating-the-quality-challenges-us-module-procurement/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

The US solar industry is facing variable module quality from new suppliers emerging due to protectionist policies, with defects rising as manufacturing hubs in Africa, the Middle East, and other regions ramp up production to serve the US market. By May 2026, the US had reached 66GW of annual module nameplate capacity, but testing laboratories like Kiwa PVEL and Kiwa PI Berlin report record-high defect rates, driven by factory ramp-up periods and cost-cutting measures such as using thinner materials and faster processing times. Experts emphasize that rigorous procurement tools—like PQP testing, factory audits, and pre-shipment inspections—are now essential to mitigate risks, as buyers are urged to leverage ample supply to enforce quality assurances and diversify suppliers.

**「Technical Details」** Quality deficiencies in modules often result from manufacturing compromises, including thinner encapsulant, frames, and glass, alongside accelerated lamination and cell processing to reduce costs, which heightens defect rates during production ramp-up. To address these issues, procurement best practices now mandate comprehensive quality assurance protocols such as Product Qualification Program \(PQP\) testing, pre-shipment inspections \(PSI\), and batch testing to verify reliability and catch defects before shipment.

**「Industry Impact」** The persistent quality issues compel buyers to adopt stricter procurement standards and renegotiate contracts, increasing project risks and costs for developers relying on newer suppliers while shifting market leverage toward those who can enforce higher reliability.
